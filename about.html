About the project

Not just a lake-cleaning robot — a plan for cleaner lakes.

Lake Sentry is an environmental technology and awareness project focused on reducing floating waste in urban lakes, starting with Hyderabad. It combines research, engineering, awareness and community action.

What it does

A dual-solution approach: remove what's there, prevent what's next.

A clean-up machine on its own only treats the symptom: if waste keeps reaching the lake, the machine is never finished. Lake Sentry pairs a physical waste collector with an awareness campaign, and connects the two through an online platform.

Prevent

Awareness campaign

Educates people about lake pollution and responsible waste disposal on Instagram, LinkedIn and through offline installations, with the aim of turning awareness into action.

Remove

Lake Sentry V1 → V2

A remote-controlled floating machine that collects waste already on the lake's surface. Lessons from V1 feed into a smarter, more reliable V2.

Connect

LakeSentry.in

A central home for research, lake pages, the public survey and a curated network of lake-cleaning initiatives. Try the demo.

The problem

Floating trash is choking Hyderabad's lakes.

Lakes are important freshwater ecosystems that support aquatic life, surrounding habitats and nearby communities. Many urban lakes, including Rangadhamuni, Malkam Cheruvu and Film Nagar Lake, are polluted by floating trash and debris. Lake Sentry focuses specifically on floating solid waste:

  • Plastic bottles
  • Plastic bags
  • Food wrappers & packaging
  • Containers
  • Glass bottles
  • Miscellaneous debris

Why it matters

Degrades water quality
Accumulated waste harms aquatic habitats and interferes with the organisms living there.
Blocks sunlight
A surface layer of trash blocks the sunlight lakes rely on for photosynthesis.
Breeds mosquitoes
Waste can obstruct water movement and create stagnant water where mosquitoes breed.
A public nuisance
Polluted lakes become an unpleasant sight and a health concern for people living nearby.

Scope note: sewage, industrial discharge and dissolved chemical pollution are real problems too, but they need separate regulatory and infrastructure solutions. Lake Sentry's scope is floating solid waste.

The root cause: improper waste disposal

  • —Lack of proper waste infrastructure
  • —Lack of public awareness
  • —Lack of care for the consequences

Motivation & research

It started with a question at the lakeside.

Visits to lakes around Hyderabad showed plastic, packaging and other waste piling up, especially where waste infrastructure seemed inadequate. That raised a question: instead of only cleaning lakes by hand after pollution builds up, could an affordable machine keep removing floating waste while awareness efforts tackle the source?

Case study: Hussain Sagar

  1. 1500s

    A man-made lake is built

    Hussain Sagar is constructed as an artificial lake.

  2. Until the 1950s

    Almost free of pollution

    The lake stays in relatively good condition for centuries.

  3. Recent decades

    Waste channels, shrinkage, biodiversity loss

    About 1,041 kg of phosphates and 1,204 kg of nitrates enter the lake every day through four waste channels, and encroachment has shrunk the lake.

Primary research

  • Public survey. 40 respondents so far on the types of waste they see, its causes, how serious it is and their support for technological clean-up.
  • Field observations & photos. Keystone International School students visited and photographed several Hyderabad lakes.
  • Expert interview. Vishal Raj, a Hyderabad environmental activist experienced in lake cleaning, said the core issue isn't ignorance: most people understand the danger but take it lightly.
  • Secondary research. Scientific literature and reports on plastic pollution, freshwater ecosystems and urban lakes in India and Hyderabad.

Remove · the physical solution

Lake Sentry V1: a working, tested prototype.

How a clean-up run works

  1. 1 · AimThe pilot steers the remote-controlled bot toward floating trash.
  2. 2 · ScoopA mesh conveyor belt lifts light and medium trash aboard.
  3. 3 · ReturnThe bot is piloted back to the lake bank.
  4. 4 · RecordTrash is weighed, photographed and timed for impact metrics.

Main components

Floating frame
CPVC/PVC pipes form the structure that holds everything else.
Buoyancy
Lightweight thermocol keeps the machine and its load afloat.
Propulsion
Two propellers on a PVC shaft, driven by DC motors, move it forward, backward and let it turn.
Conveyor
A slanted mesh belt catches floating waste and carries it upward.
Storage
A large netted compartment holds the waste and lets water drain out.

Engineering iteration: fixing the slipping conveyor

  1. ProblemThe mesh slipped on the rotating rod, so waste moved incorrectly instead of being lifted.
  2. DiagnosisThe smooth rod didn't give the mesh enough grip.
  3. ModificationThe rod was modified with extra points of friction.
  4. ResultThe mesh now grips and moves with the rod, carrying waste up into storage.

You can try both versions of the rod in the V1 simulator.

V1 → V2 · proposed, not final

Not just bigger — better.

V2 aims to be more efficient, more reliable, more environmentally compatible and more intelligent. These are the areas being explored:

  • Solar powerLonger runs with less manual charging.
  • Greater automationLess need for a human pilot.
  • SensorsNavigation, waste detection, obstacle avoidance and monitoring.
  • Better storageMore capacity and easier emptying.
  • Stronger mechanicsA more reliable conveyor, propulsion and frame.
  • Eco-friendly materialsDurable and corrosion-resistant, and gentle on aquatic life.
  • Modular designSwappable parts for different water bodies.
  • WeatherproofingA protected frame and electronics for monsoon conditions.

Prevent · the awareness solution

A bot alone can't stop pollution. The root cause is behaviour.

  • “The Rollers”: an offline installation of rotating drums with ironic anti-littering messages.
  • Online campaigns: Instagram and LinkedIn posts that lead people to LakeSentry.in, then on to the research, the survey and local initiatives.
  • Reaching young people first: starting with Hyderabad's environmental creators and college eco-clubs, with a joint lake clean-up day as the first shared activity.

Who it's for

Not a consumer product: a tool for the people who look after lakes.

The machine is meant for organisations that manage lakes. The awareness campaign speaks to the wider public. These are two different audiences with one shared goal.

  • Government & municipal bodies

    Lake-management authorities such as GHMC and HYDRAA, and partners for site access and permissions.

  • Environmental NGOs

    Groups already restoring lakes. Lake Sentry aims to join their efforts, not duplicate them.

  • CSR & nonprofits

    Potential funders to help manufacture and scale once the model is proven.

  • Local communities

    Residents who live with the effects of polluted lakes, some of whom could be trained as volunteer operators.

UN Sustainable Development Goals

Where Lake Sentry contributes

SDG 6 · Clean Water & Sanitation

  • 6.3: improve water quality by removing floating solid waste.
  • 6.6: protect and restore water-related ecosystems, including lakes.
  • 6.b: community participation through the campaign and initiative network.

SDG 11 · Sustainable Cities

  • 11.6: reduce the environmental impact of cities through lake clean-up.
  • 11.a: water and waste systems connect urban and surrounding areas.

SDG 12 · Responsible Consumption

Encouraging responsible disposal, less littering and awareness of what happens to our waste.

SDG 14 · Life Below Water (supporting)

Less floating plastic in freshwater protects aquatic life and means less waste travelling downstream.

Lake Sentry contributes to and aligns with these goals; it does not claim to achieve them on its own.
