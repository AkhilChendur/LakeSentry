01 · Squeezed by the city

Lakes shrink as the city builds into them

Homes, roads and walls built inside a lake's full tank level (FTL) or its buffer zone reduce the area that can hold water, and cut off the channels that feed it.

14,061encroachments found around 134 lakes within GHMC limits

Irrigation Department report to the National Green Tribunal (Nov 2022), as reported by The News Minute. A state study (TGRAC) reported 491 of 920 lakes and tanks encroached over 20 years (The South First).

02 · A green carpet

Water hyacinth covers the surface

Water hyacinth is an invasive floating plant. Fed by nutrients in sewage, it spreads into thick mats. They look green and healthy, but they shade the water below, trap floating rubbish and choke the life underneath.

How hyacinth mats affect a lake: Vermont Agency of Agriculture fact sheet; Louisiana Sea Grant fact sheet.

03 · The root cause

Sewage pours in through the nalas

Untreated sewage and industrial effluent reach lakes through stormwater channels called nalas. The nitrates and phosphates they carry feed algae and water hyacinth, so the weeds grow back faster than they can be cleared.

Root causewaste and sewage entering upstream, not just what floats on top

Causes of Hussain Sagar's pollution: HMDA. A February 2026 review found untreated and partly treated sewage still flowing into several lakes: SANDRP.

04 · Ecosystem breakdown

Oxygen runs out, and fish die

As weeds and waste rot, bacteria use up the oxygen dissolved in the water. Fish need at least 4 mg/l (the Central Pollution Control Board's minimum for fisheries).

0.3 mg/ldissolved oxygen at Durgam Cheruvu in pollution board figures released in May 2026, when hundreds of fish died

Telangana Pollution Control Board figures, as reported by Deccan Chronicle. In March 2025, 24 of 185 lakes in Greater Hyderabad were described as “dead lakes” because of very low oxygen (Deccan Chronicle).

05 · Sunlight blocked

Underneath, it goes dark

Below a thick mat, little sunlight gets through. Rooted plants can't photosynthesise and die, so they stop making oxygen, and their decay uses up even more. Fish and other life leave, or die.

Mat blocks lightPlants below dieDecay uses oxygenLife collapses

Vermont Agency of Agriculture; Louisiana Sea Grant.

06 · Out of sight

Sludge and sunken waste on the bed

Heavier waste sinks: bottles, cans, sandals, tyres. Rotting organic waste settles as dark sludge, where pollutants can build up for years.

3 mhow deep heavy metals had seeped into Hussain Sagar's lake bed, according to NIT Warangal research

As reported by The News Minute (study date not confirmed).

07 · Dumped on the banks

Debris and rubbish pile up on the shore

Construction debris and household waste dumped at a lake's edge slowly fill it in. Every rain washes more plastic into the water.

From our own visits: the Lake Sentry team has seen floating waste at lakes such as Rangadhamuni, Malkam Cheruvu and Film Nagar.

08 · A health risk

Weed-choked water breeds mosquitoes

Doctors with the Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme have described Hyderabad's most polluted, hyacinth-choked lakes as high-density breeding grounds for mosquitoes that carry dengue, chikungunya and malaria.

As reported by Telangana Today (July 2026).

09 · What can change

Clear it, and stop it arriving

Lake Sentry is a low-cost floating robot that scoops waste off the surface (shown here as an artist's impression of V1). The lasting fix starts upstream: less rubbish and less untreated sewage reaching the water.

  1. Never throw waste near a lake or into a nala.
  2. Report dumping and encroachment to GHMC or HYDRAA.
  3. Join a clean-up, or share what you see at your lake.

About this prototype

  • Prototype. Lake Dive is an early, experimental part of the Lake Sentry website. It may change, contain mistakes, or be switched off at any time.
  • Not a real lake. The lake, its shape, the buildings, plants, animals and waste are all generated by computer code. It does not depict any specific lake, place, building or property, and it does not show the current condition of any lake.
  • Imitating real conditions. The scene loosely imitates conditions that have been reported at urban lakes in and around Hyderabad: water hyacinth, sewage inflows, floating and sunken waste, dumping and encroachment. Amounts, positions, colours, depths and distances are illustrative and not to scale.
  • The gauges are illustrations. The Sunlight and Oxygen bars show the idea of each chapter. They are not measurements.
  • Facts and figures. Every figure is credited to the report or news outlet that published it, with the date where we know it. Figures may have changed since. For some, we could only read news coverage, not the original report, and we have not independently verified every number. Please check the original source before quoting it.
  • No claims about anyone. Buildings, channels and dumping in the scene are generic illustrations, not claims about any real building, owner, organisation or authority.
  • The robot. The Lake Sentry robot shown is an artist's impression of our V1 prototype. The real robot looks and performs differently.
  • Not advice. This is an awareness tool, not scientific, legal, health or safety advice. For official information, contact the Telangana Pollution Control Board, GHMC, HYDRAA or HMDA.
  • Photos. Real photographs, when we add them, will be clearly labelled with the lake's name and the date.
  • Corrections. Spotted something wrong? Tell us through the contact form.
