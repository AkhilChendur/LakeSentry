About Lake Sentry

A robot that cleans lakes. A campaign that keeps them clean.

Lake Sentry is a student-led project that removes floating trash from Hyderabad's urban lakes, and works to stop it getting there in the first place.

The problem

Floating trash is choking Hyderabad's lakes.

Bottles, bags, wrappers, containers and glass collect on the surface of lakes such as Rangadhamuni, Malkam Cheruvu and Film Nagar Lake. The root cause is improper waste disposal: too little infrastructure, awareness and care.

  • 🐟 Harms habitatsand degrades water quality
  • ☀️ Blocks sunlightthat lakes need to thrive
  • 🦟 Breeds mosquitoesin stagnant, blocked water
  • 🏘️ Spoils lakesfor the people living nearby

Scope: floating solid waste. Sewage, industrial discharge and chemical pollution need separate solutions.

Motivation & research

It started with a question at the lakeside.

Instead of only cleaning lakes by hand after pollution builds up, could an affordable machine keep removing floating waste, while awareness efforts tackle the source?

Visits to lakes around Hyderabad showed plastic, packaging and other waste piling up, especially where waste-management infrastructure seemed inadequate. That question became the foundation of Lake Sentry.

See the survey results

Interview notes: Vishal Raj, environmental activist

What is your approach to cleaning water bodies like lakes?
Look at what a lake needs to survive. The banks need to be strong, so the depth isn't affected and the surface area doesn't shrink.
What is the root cause of floating pollution?
People are mostly aware of the problem but take it lightly, as if it's not a big deal.

This matches the survey: the core issue isn't ignorance. Most people already understand the danger. What's missing is the will to act on it.

Remove · the physical solution

Lake Sentry V1: a working prototype.

A remote-controlled floating machine built from CPVC pipe, thermocol floats and a mesh conveyor. Simple, affordable and locally buildable.

  2. 02 · ScoopThe mesh conveyor lifts it aboard.
  3. 03 · ReturnPilot back to the bank.

What's next

Better, not just bigger.

V2 ideas (proposed, not final): more efficient, reliable, eco-friendly and intelligent.

  • ☀️ Solar power
  • 🤖 Automation
  • 📡 Sensors
  • 🗑️ More storage
  • ⚙️ Sturdier build
  • 🌱 Eco-friendly materials
  • 🧩 Modular parts
  • 🌧️ Weatherproofing

A bot alone can't stop pollution. The root cause is behaviour. So alongside the robot, “The Rollers” installation and Instagram and LinkedIn campaigns spread the message, starting with Hyderabad's young people and college eco-clubs and leading back to LakeSentry.in.

Who it's for

The people who look after lakes.

Not a consumer product. The robot is a tool for organisations that manage lakes, and the campaign speaks to the wider public.

  • 🏛️ Government and lake authorities (GHMC, HYDRAA)
  • 🏙️ Municipal bodies
  • 🌱 Environmental NGOs
  • 💼 CSR and nonprofits
  • 👥 Local communities and volunteers

More details

Expected impact and how we measure it
  • Environment: cut floating trash per km² by 50% or more per lake compared with the first baseline clean-ups; track habitat cleanliness with photos, video and drone imaging; track bird and wildlife species with local partners.
  • Society: build partnerships with nonprofits, CSR programmes and local government; grow public awareness; scale clean-ups across the region.
  • Core metric: weight of trash recovered per km² for each lake cleaned, compared across lakes to show which need the most attention.
Risks and how we plan for them
RiskMitigation
No standing permission to operate on municipal lakesPartner with HYDRAA / GHMC and lakeside resident associations before each new lake.
The monsoon halts clean-ups and can damage electronicsSchedule clean-ups outside peak monsoon months; weatherproof the CPVC frame and electronics housing.
An unattended bot at the lake bank could be damaged or stolenStore it off-site between clean-ups and operate only in supervised sessions until a lockable dock is affordable.
Scaling to more lakes needs more than one operatorTrain volunteer operators from partner nonprofits and eco-clubs during the pilot phase.
An honest feasibility check
DimensionCurrent status
TechnicalV1 demonstrates basic flotation, propulsion, remote control and waste collection in controlled conditions.
EconomicThe prototype uses simple, accessible components. Costs haven't yet been formally compared with commercial systems.
OperationalBattery life, storage capacity and operator needs are still being measured through testing.
EnvironmentalEffects on aquatic organisms, material safety and disposal of collected waste need further evaluation.
SocialSurvey data shows strong public support for technological clean-up (80% of 40 respondents).
InstitutionalRelevance to NGOs and government lake bodies is a hypothesis to test through outreach and pilots.

Lake Sentry does not claim full real-world feasibility until enough field testing has been done.
